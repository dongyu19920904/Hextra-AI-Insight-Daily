### 4. FrontierHarness v1.1: When You Switch Models, Switch Your Harness Too

**Rankings shift dramatically when the same harness is paired with a different model.** The [FrontierHarness Eval v1.1 report](https://runta.com/blog/introducing-frontierharness-eval-v1-1/) used 83B tokens and **27,280** agent runs to complete a test matrix of 22 harnesses × 5 models × 62 tasks. The key finding: **Codex CLI** ranked first in cost under GLM-5.3 but dropped to 16th under Kimi-K3. The median harness shifted 11 positions in cost ranking across different models. Teams currently evaluating coding agent setups will find this data worth examining.

![FrontierHarness v1.1 original chart](https://runta.com/blog/introducing-frontierharness-eval-v1-1/harness-scatter-all-models.png "FrontierHarness v1.1: original article chart")

### 6. AI Model Costs Falling Roughly 47% Per Quarter

**The pace of cost reduction exceeds most technology curves.** [Epoch AI data summarized by Weixi](https://m.okjike.com/originalPosts/6afba0ab136b2761c6bb2888) shows that since 2023, the cost of equivalent model intelligence has dropped roughly **47% per quarter**, which works out to approximately **13× per year**. That rate is 4× faster than DNA sequencing and 6× faster than compute chips. Teams currently budgeting for AI tools may find that this year's cost assumptions are already significantly out of date.

### 9. LobeHub Introduces an Experience Layer for Agent Memory

**A new approach addresses the problem of agents losing lessons between tasks.** [Jike user Max_means_best shared](https://m.okjike.com/originalPosts/6afbdf24e04066f7ed094998) details of LobeHub's self-evolution feature: agents write successful paths and failure lessons from each task into an **Experience Layer**, rather than relying solely on Skill files. Testing shows the mechanism can handle certain task types that were previously difficult to solve. The feature is still being iterated on, and the author notes it does not yet **cover all task scenarios**.

### Saved 55%, But First You Have to Read the Manual

Grok Bot got the **Super** upgrade — 15× the quota — only to find that **75%** of it was burning on scheduled tasks that ran regardless of whether anything had happened. No updates, still runs for half an hour, still delivers a solemn report: "[No major updates at this time; here is a current progress overview](https://m.okjike.com/originalPosts/6afbda91136b2761c6c1e3f9)." After copying the "don't fire if nothing changed" logic from Anthropic's always-on agent guide into the prompt, quota dropped by **55%**. Step one of saving money: admit how much you were wasting before.
