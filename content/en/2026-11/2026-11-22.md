### 1. Claude Independently Solves Two Open Problems in Black Hole Physics

**AI participates in mathematical physics proofs for the first time.** 📐 Anthropic [Claude independently solves two open problems in black hole physics](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics), the **Claude** agent ran for about **six weeks**, solving two problems: the sign of phase transitions on the quantum side of holographic duality, and the upper bound on the mass of the lightest black hole in 3D AdS quantum gravity. The modular bootstrap results were formally verified via Lean, and the main conclusions were independently reviewed by physicists. These are calculations within a theoretical model and do not directly describe observable black holes; behavior in the intermediate coupling regime also remains undetermined.

### 8. AI-Agent-Designed Frostbite CPU Reports 4.59x Performance Per Watt in Pre-Tapeout Data

**AI involved throughout CPU architecture exploration.** 📄 Architect Labs [introduces on their blog](https://architectlabs.com/blog/frostbite) Frostbite, where the team had AI agents explore the compiler, architecture, RTL, and physical layout in parallel; the 7nm design reached **500 MHz** in the final GDS. Four compute clusters compared against a dual-issue in-order core of similar area, the geometric mean of performance per watt across 25 workloads was **4.59x**, with the held-out evaluation group at 3.83x. These are pre-tapeout data, the comparison core is unnamed, and an out-of-order core and standard benchmark suite are still planned.

### 10. Mani Doraisamy: When AI Writes Code, Developers Must Own Architecture Trade-offs

**AI-generated code pushes architecture decisions back to developers.** 💻 **Mani** Doraisamy [documents on his personal blog](https://manidoraisamy.com/developer-to-architect.html) that while debugging a Google Forms plugin, he found that AI-generated installation logs triggered multiple Google API calls sequentially, causing the welcome page to take about **15 seconds** to load. He ultimately chose to only log the installation time and display the welcome page as early as possible, and argues that when architecture changes are involved, tools should first obtain consent before writing implementations. This is the author's assessment of a single incident and cannot be used to conclude that coding jobs will disappear.
