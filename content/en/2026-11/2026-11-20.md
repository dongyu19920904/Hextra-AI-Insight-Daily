## **🔥 Today's Top 10**

### 1. Google Releases Gemini 4 Neon, Designed for Fast Completion of Complex Tasks

**New model Neon goes live.** The official Google account announced that [Gemini 4 Neon is now rolling out to paid subscribers](https://x.com/GeminiApp/status/2123174195848945768), covering Google AI Pro and Ultra users as well as equivalent Gemini App subscriptions. The model is designed for multi-file debugging, code refactoring, and multi-step instruction following, with Google claiming task completion speeds roughly **40%** faster than the previous generation. Third-party evaluations show token prices down about **25%** and per-task costs down around 17%. Users running code or complex workflows can switch today to try it out.

### 2. Tetra-1 Folds Protein Structures Directly into Its Chain of Thought, Surpassing AlphaFold3

**A significant step for biological reasoning.** Jiqizhixin reports that SimpleX has opened [Tetra-1 Preview for trial use](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s?__biz=MzA3MzI4MjgzMw==&mid=2651075302&idx=1&sn=1badc9c3cd5176dcd98e705345707a2d). According to the report, it surpasses **AlphaFold3**, IsoDDE, and Claude Opus on protein folding benchmarks. This reduces information loss from passing data between systems, and researchers in structural biology and drug development should take note.

### 4. Third-Party Evaluation: Gemini 4 Neon's Coding Costs Half Those of Competitors

**Comparable coding performance at a substantially lower price.** [Artificial Analysis benchmark data](https://www.bestblogs.dev/en/status/2123170645777526962?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=feed&utm_campaign=resources&entry=rss_article_item) shows Neon matches Claude Code and Fable 5.2 on the coding agent index, but costs just **$6.86 per task** compared to **$12.61** for Claude Code and $15.47 for Fable 5.2, a difference of more than double. Its intelligence index score is 61, below Fable 5.2's 63 and GPT-6.2 Astra's 62. For cost-sensitive batch coding tasks, Neon currently offers the best value.

### 7. Ruan Yifeng: 2026 Is the Most Distracted Year Yet

**Multi-agent parallelism is changing how individuals work.** In [Weekly Issue 420](http://www.ruanyifeng.com/blog/2026/11/weekly-issue-420.html), Ruan Yifeng cites Hugging Face evangelist Alejandro AO's year-in-review, in which Alejandro describes running "four to ten agents simultaneously across three or four projects" in 2026, burning through **millions of tokens** per day. Ruan characterizes this as the **"cost of focus"** that AI brings: running multiple projects in parallel has become easier, while sustaining deep concentration on a single task has become harder.

### 10. AI Exploration Guide Tests Readability Improvements in AI Long-Form Translation

**Prompt direction has a greater impact on translation quality than model choice.** The author of Telegram channel AI Exploration Guide published [a testing log](https://t.me/aigc1024/27226) addressing the problem of AI translations of long passages that read unnaturally. Using a controlled approach with multiple free models and different instruction combinations, they achieved a usable result with **THUDM/GLM**-4-9B-0414 paired with a custom prompt. The conclusion is that prompt direction matters more than model selection. Users who struggle with stiff AI translations can refer to the prompt framework they published.
