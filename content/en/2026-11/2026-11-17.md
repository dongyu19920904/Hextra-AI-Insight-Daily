### 1. Tencent Hy4-Flash Goes Open Source at ~1/18 the Cost of Competitors

**Open-source multimodal model released.** Tencent [published Hy4-Flash's architecture and benchmark results](https://www.bestblogs.dev/en/status/2122389243628794016?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=feed&utm_campaign=resources&entry=rss_article_item): 224B total parameters with only **12B** activated per token, supporting a 1-million-token context window. A blind evaluation with 85 participants across 259 everyday tasks yielded a satisfaction score of 2.79/4, close to Claude Haiku's 2.81, at a per-task cost of roughly ¥0.26. The KV cache is **1/24** the size of Hy3's. Developers running batch workloads can try it now on WorkBuddy's free tier.

### 2. Qwen4-Max Launches with Per-Task Cost Down to $1.67

**Alibaba's flagship model receives a major update.** The [Qwen official blog](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen4-max) details the architecture: a 2.4-trillion-parameter backbone with **71 billion** parameters activated per token, supporting a million-token context. Internal Work360 benchmarks show average per-task cost fell from $4.43 to **$1.67**, a drop of roughly 62%. A Live Delivery feature lets long tasks yield usable drafts early; the Live Delivery plugin is open source, though the Max weights themselves have not been released.

### 10. Codex's Three Permission Levels: Full Access Runs Smoothly but Keeps Developers "On Edge"

**Developers share honest feedback on Codex's tool-call permission tiers.** A V2EX user [documented the day-to-day experience with all three permission levels](https://www.v2ex.com/t/1257520#reply9): Ask for approval interrupts constantly, Approve for me still pauses on tool calls, and **Full** Access runs smoothly but has **hard-to-predict** boundaries. This is a real trade-off between automation and control, with no universally correct answer. Developers new to Codex should start with Approve for me, get a feel for the model's behavior, and only then decide whether to increase the permission level.
