## **🔥 Today's Top 10 Highlights**

### 1. Anthropic Releases Cryptographic Proof Technology for Verifiable Reasoning Provenance

**Reasoning provenance can now be mathematically proven.** An official **Anthropic** tweet announced that [FlashProve is based on mature cryptographic techniques](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2117710942826123294), enabling developers to verify whether a given response truly came from a specified AI model. The research claims its efficiency **significantly outperforms** existing open-source methods. These proofs can be used to prevent attackers from hijacking conversations, and also allow security evaluators to verify model identity.

### 2. Tencent Hunyuan Hyra 2.0 Lets AI Agents Autonomously Develop Scientific Research Tools

**AI can now train models and fix chip defects on its own.** A report from the Tencent team on [Hyra 2.0's scientific research system](https://x.com/Gorden_Sun/status/2117625758814474746) describes a three-layer agent collaboration framework called Harness, which distills debugging experience into a reusable, standardized skill library. According to the team, tools already produced include HyInv, a protein design model with a generation speed **57 times faster** than commonly used industry tools, and HyProver, a mathematical proof workflow that the team claims has **completely proven** Yau's circle action conjecture. The project is not currently open source.

### 3. Google's Hard Bundle: Schools That Ban AI Will Also Lose Student Search Access

**Schools that reject AI may also lose student search access.** The New York Times [reports on Google's new arrangement for education accounts](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/03/us/google-search-schools-restrict-ai.html): starting in early **2027**, AI tools will be off by default for K–12 student accounts, but schools that choose to fully restrict AI will simultaneously lose **Google** Search access for student accounts. Schools that allow AI can choose between two modes: a consumer version that retains AI overviews, or a Gemini student-guided learning version. The Los Angeles school district had previously hoped to keep search while disabling AI summaries; under the arrangement described in the report, schools would struggle to preserve both options at once.

### 8. RF-Sensing Glasses Convert Silent Lip Movements to Text in Real Time

**Mouth your words silently, and the glasses can recognize what you're saying.** The Subvocal Company (YC F26) presented their smart glasses prototype in a [Hacker News Show HN post](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340088): antennas in the frame emit radio frequency signals that capture tongue, lip, and soft palate movements, which a model then decodes into text. ### Ubuntu Has Not Delivered an Official Zero-Day Fix Kernel Publicly for Three Weeks

**The RCE zero-day patch is still in the testing repository and has not entered the regular update channel.** A Freenode article [documents the fix progress for CVE-2026-98432](https://freenode.net/article/ubuntu-lts-kernels-still-unfixed-for-critical-cve-2026-98432): Ubuntu's official statement from November 2 shows that fixed kernels for 22.04, 24.04, and 26.04 LTS have entered the `-proposed` testing repository and are being validated on over two thousand certified servers and devices before moving to the regular update channel. 26.10 has already been fixed in 7.3.0-10.10; older supported versions are still pending. The vulnerability is officially confirmed to allow **remote code execution** and is not limited to MPTCP paths — disabling MPTCP is not a complete mitigation. Ops and server administrators can now manually enable the `-proposed` repository to test the patch early, or wait for it to be pushed through the stable channel.

## **◉ Social Media Picks**

### AI Coding Assistant Self-Retrospective Effectiveness: Only 2 Out of 7 Tests Actually Worked

**Having AI revise its own working manual after finishing a task is far less impressive in practice than it sounds.** Baoyu's tweet cited [paper research results from Singapore Management University and Heidelberg University](https://x.com/dotey/status/2117706197797114009): out of 7 valid tests, only **2** changes completely eliminated the target problem, and some changes caused the AI to perform unnecessary checks on unrelated tasks. Baoyu himself admitted he has always been skeptical of this kind of "self-evolving Skill" — without clear evaluation criteria, AI struggles to self-optimize effectively; relying entirely on human review is also costly. Developers currently using `/retro` or similar self-iteration Skills can reference this data to lower their expectations.
