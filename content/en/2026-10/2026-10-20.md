### 2. Mistral Releases Timestral, a Time-Series Model That Runs on CPU

**Timestral supports standalone CPU inference.** [Mistral's announcement of Timestral](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/) introduces four model sizes — 5M, 28M, 123M, and 209M — for time-series foundation modeling, with a context window of up to **32,768** time steps and **21** quantile outputs. The 209M model takes roughly 140 milliseconds to forecast one day ahead on three weeks of 15-minute-granularity data, running on a standard server CPU with eight threads. Developers who need business forecasting can evaluate whether this can replace their existing specialized algorithm pipelines.

### 9. CERN and Mistral Sign Letter of Intent to Develop a Large Physics Model

**Particle-physics data may be used to train a dedicated foundation model.** According to [CERN's announcement](https://home.cern/cern-and-mistral-join-forces-to-accelerate-ai-for-science/), the two parties aim to train a Large Physics Model on simulated proton-collision data from the Large Hadron Collider, targeting applications in collision reconstruction and particle identification. CERN has already stored approximately 1 EB (one million TB) of LHC experimental data; the High-Luminosity upgrade is expected to come online in **2030**. **This is a letter of intent** — final model scale, licensing terms, and delivery timeline have not been announced. A statement of intent is not the same as a launched project.

## **◎ Industry Shifts and Personal Impact**

### Smarter Models Will Absorb Old-Style Coding Assistance, But Not the Harness

**Rising model capabilities are redrawing the boundaries of engineering.** In a [talk summary from Jediah Katz at AI Engineer](https://www.bestblogs.dev/en/video/1a8557eea?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=feed&utm_campaign=resources&entry=rss_article_item), drawn from his experience working on the Cursor agent harness, he argues that frontier models have already internalized many coding tricks that once required dedicated prompting and guardrails — but that runtime reliability, permission security, and cost control still need to be handled by an external harness. Cloud execution and fine-grained permissions remain the responsibility of external systems and will not be absorbed by the model. Developers doing agent engineering can use this to judge which prompt-engineering investments are still worth continuing and which should be handed off to runtime infrastructure.
