### 4. Poetry Characters Arranged Into Readable Images

**AI turns a poem into a picture made entirely of its own text.** Li Jigang [shared the complete prompt method on Jike](https://m.okjike.com/originalPosts/6add7f4bbd0563695be92767) 📝. The position and direction of the characters form the image itself. The process runs in four steps: identify the objects and relationships in the poem, define the spatial projection, arrange the characters into a picture, and check each element. Verification criteria include that **every character in the image comes from the original poem**, that the image is recognizable, and that **the poem can be read back by following the path**. Three example output images are included. Suitable for parents who want to use AI to help their children learn classical poetry.

### Cerebras Deploys a Three-Trillion-Parameter Model Across 59 Wafers

**A single wafer's SRAM can't hold it all — layers are distributed across multiple wafers.** [Cerebras uses Kimi K3 as a case study to explain pipeline deployment](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/how-we-deploy-trillion-parameter-frontier-models-on-cerebras) 🔥. Assumptions: weights at **4-bit quantization**, KV cache at 8-bit. Each layer's weights are approximately 16 GB. The 69 linear-attention layers are arranged at two layers per wafer. The 24 full-attention layers are placed at one layer per wafer, requiring **59 wafers** in total. Weights reside on-chip permanently; activations are passed between wafers via Ethernet RDMA. Each hop adds roughly 2 microseconds of overhead.

### Codex Memory Can Be Enabled in One Direction Only

Someone digging through Codex's desktop config files discovered that [the memory feature is actually split into two independent switches: "generate memories" and "use memories"](https://www.v2ex.com/t/1251733#reply0). This means you can let the AI quietly accumulate information in Project A while **injecting no historical memory** to keep conversations clean; then, when you switch to a research project, flip `use_memories = true` and let it **actively judge** whether what it gathered is relevant to your workflow. It's like a **piggy bank that only takes deposits** — you let it fill up, then tip it all out at once when you're ready.
