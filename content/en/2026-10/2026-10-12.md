### 1. MIT Research: AI Digital Twins Lose Accuracy in New Contexts

**Cross-context prediction is shaky.** HugAgent was proposed by MIT and affiliated institutions. According to [a research summary compiled by 36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/4020977744449666), the study covered **54 participants** and **1,742 evaluation items**, testing major models including GPT, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek. The core finding: AI learns how you think *right now*, but throw a brand-new context at it and prediction accuracy drops noticeably. The paper has been accepted to EMNLP 2026 Main Oral. If your team is building synthetic respondents or personalized agents, it's time to reassess the ceiling on this technical approach.

### 2. Zhejiang University Open-Sources nanoMuse: Send Tasks from Phone, Execute on PC

**Meta Muse now has a self-hostable alternative.** According to the [nanoMuse project intro on Juejin](https://juejin.cn/post/7694883716313759795), the Zhejiang University team drew inspiration from Meta's personal agent architecture released in September and built an open-source version. The workflow: send a task from your phone, have your online PC execute it, then push results and pending approvals back to your phone 🤖. Meta's original version provisions a **dedicated cloud VM per user**; the open-source version puts resource scheduling in your own hands. Developers researching personal agent architecture now have a **locally runnable reference implementation** to dig into.

### 5. After AI-Driven Layoffs, Employees Start Hiding Their Best Workflows

**Efficiency gains can be canceled out by org culture.** Baoyu referenced a Japanese blogger's take in a post: [Toyota's "kaizen" rule is that saved time never leads to headcount cuts](https://x.com/dotey/status/2109413992280039441). The logic is simple — if suggesting improvements means putting colleagues out of a job, nobody on the floor will ever speak up again. He argues AI adoption follows the same pattern: the moment **"efficiency = layoffs"** becomes the signal, employees will actively hide their most productive workflows. Managers leading AI rollouts need to design incentive structures that head this off before it starts.

### Claude subscribers want to use AI to simulate their own judgment style — is one training session enough?

**It's not about the number of sessions — it's that the moment a new context appears, the model loses its anchor to "you."** [This MIT study](https://www.36kr.com/p/4020977744449666) tested 54 real participants and found that even with thorough interviews, model prediction accuracy drops noticeably the moment an unseen scenario appears. Rather than betting on a single interview to cover every possible situation, a better move is to distill your stances and reasoning patterns from typical scenarios into reusable prompt snippets and bring them into each conversation.
