## Direct Conclusion

- **Direct answer:** The most worthwhile thing to validate today is turning the action of "migrating fixed-label classification tasks from large models to small models" into comparison samples that small and medium-sized teams can understand, then asking whether they would pay for those results.
- **Do it or not:** Build the sample first, then decide after seeing real feedback within 48 hours.
- **Validate first:** Run one fixed-label classification comparison, show the results to 3–5 people with recurring classification needs, and observe whether anyone follows up asking about price.
- **When to stop:** Five people view the sample and none ask about price, or you cannot get the classification comparison running yourself within 48 hours.

## Today's Main Recommendation

### Validate Whether Fixed-Label Classification Migration Samples Can Be Sold to Small Teams With Recurring Classification Needs

**This is not news about large companies cutting costs — it is a concrete migration action that can be scaled down to small and medium-sized teams.** Strands published today the full account of how Amazon moved its ticket classification from Sonnet 4.6 to the Gemma 4 small model. The task structure is fixed (choose one label from 20), and the technical barrier is low.

- **Evidence and confidence:** Medium. [Strands official case study: a description of Amazon migrating ticket classification from Sonnet 4.6 to the Gemma 4 small model exists](https://strandsagents.com/blog/amazon-saves-millions-replacing-sonnet-with-strands-decider/). Gap: no interviews with or pricing inquiries from small teams were extracted from today's candidate inputs; "someone would pay for a migration service" remains an **unvalidated assumption**.
- **Target pool and ground-level approach:** Unvalidated assumption: there may be a class of small teams (customer-service tickets, content moderation, form classification) currently running fixed-label tasks on large models but without anyone dedicated to evaluating whether a smaller model could replace them.
- **Minimum deliverable:** Product name: "Fixed-Label Classification Migration Comparison Package." Contents: ① run the buyer's 10–20 real samples through both a large model and a small model; ② compare the accuracy of both sets of results; ③ a one-page feasibility note (suitable scenarios, unsuitable scenarios, boundary conditions). Acceptance criterion: the buyer can see two sets of classification results on their own data plus one readable explanation. Excludes production deployment, API integration, and ongoing maintenance.
- **48-hour validation:** First, run the small model on 20 self-prepared samples once, record the actual time taken and any failure points, and save screenshots as your sample. Then send those screenshots to 3–5 people who have mentioned "using AI for classification or moderation" in a community or private conversation, and observe whether anyone follows up asking about price or spontaneously describes their own situation.
- **First sale and repeat business:** Test outreach message: "What are you using to run classification tasks right now? I built a small-model comparison sample — takes 10 minutes to see whether it's worth switching." First sale fixed scope: one comparison report using the buyer's real samples, excluding deployment and integration; set the price after testing it yourself. Repeat business currently looks more like one-off work; if the buyer confirms the results are usable, the process could be condensed into a reusable component, but that needs separate validation.
- **Risks and stopping points:** Compliance risk is low — the source is Strands' own blog and uses publicly available models; check whether the buyer's data can be used for testing. Stop if: you cannot get one complete classification comparison running yourself within 48 hours; or five target users view the sample and none ask about price.

## This Week's Small Test

### Validate Whether a Behavioral Explanation of Personal Gemini Connections to Gmail/Drive Can Be Delivered as a Small Information Package

- **Evidence source:** [gemini_files repository: a project in which the author observed Gemini's context-retrieval behavior through follow-up questions exists](https://github.com/thisisme6598/gemini_files/)
- **Target pool:** Unvalidated assumption: freelancers who have already connected their personal Gmail or Drive to Gemini and **may** be willing to pay for an explanation if they have concerns about where their data goes.
- **Minimum deliverable:** Use your own account to reproduce the connection flow, record which types of requests trigger retrieval and which do not, and compile a one-page "Personal Gemini Gmail/Drive Connection: Observable Behavior Guide" with steps for turning it off. Verifiable result for the buyer: the steps in the guide can be reproduced in their own account.
- **48-hour validation:** Complete the sample guide first, then send it to 3–5 people who use Gemini to handle work files, and observe whether anyone spontaneously forwards it or asks how to turn the connection off.
- **Why this is only a small test:** The repository has no license declaration and the source is a personal project; the author explicitly states their method cannot prove that files have been reviewed by a human; if Google publishes an official statement that differs, the guide's content could need correction at any time.
- **Stopping conditions:** You cannot reproduce the retrieval behavior the author describes in your own account; or none of the five people indicate any recognition or curiosity about the guide's content.
