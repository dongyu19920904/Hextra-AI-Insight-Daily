## Today's Main Pick

### Run a Long-Document Structured Analysis Package with Gemini 4 Argon — Stand In for Research Assistants or Content Planners

**Google officially released Gemini 4 Argon today, with a focus on long-range multi-file processing. This gives "running through large volumes of raw material in one pass and outputting structured results" a verifiable delivery boundary.** It's worth spending 48 hours testing whether anyone will pay for "run it for me and give me the results."

- **Evidence and confidence:** Medium. [The Google DeepMind official release page confirms the existence of Gemini 4 Argon and its long-context, multi-file processing capabilities](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/). All official showcase cases come from the vendor and partner reports and cannot be generalized as real-world results for arbitrary use cases. **Still missing:** This round of candidate inputs includes no real-user test records from Chinese users, no API pricing details, and no paid interviews — willingness to pay remains an unvalidated assumption.

- **The pond and the slow approach:** Unvalidated assumption: research assistants, content planners, or independent consultants may process large volumes of raw text every week, with their current slow approach being to sort through it manually in batches — producing inconsistent formats that are hard to reuse.

- **Minimum deliverable:** Product name: "Long-Document Structured Analysis Package (single run)." Deliverables: ① Accept 10–30 raw documents; ② produce a unified-format summary + key conclusions + comparison table; ③ deliver a ready-to-use Markdown or spreadsheet file; ④ include a brief guide on reusing the output. Acceptance criteria: consistent formatting, key information is searchable, no further manual sorting required. **Not included:** data collection, translation, or follow-up updates.

- **48-hour validation:** First, select 10 articles on the same topic, run a sample yourself, and record the actual time taken. Then send the sample to 3–5 target users and ask them: how long does it take them to handle a similar task, does this output save them time, and would they give a ballpark price. Someone proactively following up on pricing counts as a valid signal — saves-to-favorites does not.

- **First sale and repeat business:** Test pitch: "I'm testing an approach that organizes 10–20 documents you have on hand into a unified format in one pass — take a look at the sample first, and if it works for you, give me a ballpark price." First sale: fixed scope of 10–30 documents, one-time delivery, **not including** follow-up updates or custom fields. If the same user has a similar monthly task, this can evolve into a recurring organizing service; when demand is similar enough, it can be packaged as a fixed-format template bundle for resale.

- **Risks and stopping:** Risk is **low**. Commercial use of the API requires you to independently verify Google's terms of use. Stop conditions: 5 users review the sample and no one asks about pricing; actual production cost exceeds what buyers are willing to pay; or the current use case proves non-compliant after checking the license.
