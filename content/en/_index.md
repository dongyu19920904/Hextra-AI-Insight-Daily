## **🔥 Today's Top 10 Highlights**

### 1. Anthropic Plans Nasdaq IPO Under Ticker ANT

**IPO filing is now public.** The [Anthropic official IPO page](https://ipo.anthropic.com/us/) shows the company plans to issue approximately **825 million** Class A shares at a price range of $70–$78 per share. Pricing is expected on November 18, with trading set to begin on November 19, subject to regulatory approval and market conditions. Q3 2026 revenue was $17 billion, with an adjusted operating margin of positive **15%**; the net loss of $23.3 billion includes $21.9 billion in non-cash valuation changes. The company will retain its public benefit corporation status, with the founders' entity controlling approximately 50.1% of general voting rights through Class F shares.

### 3. Fei-Fei Li Releases Atlas Unified World Model

**World models move toward robot training.** According to 36Kr, Fei-Fei Li delivered a talk at CoRL 2026 presenting the [Atlas unified world model](https://www.36kr.com/p/4066226367632263), built on a multimodal autoregressive diffusion Transformer architecture. This was the first public appearance of Fei-Fei Li and Lisa Su on the same stage following AMD's announcement of its approximately **$8.2 billion** acquisition of World Labs. Li disclosed that Atlas is being tested for its practical value in **robotic simulation training** and is capable of generating and reconstructing three-dimensional environments.

### 4. ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview Weights to Be Open-Sourced Before November 25

**Baidu's new ERNIE version makes its debut.** According to AIBase, [ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview weights are set to be open-sourced before November 25](https://www.aibase.com/zh/news/32282), with **280B** total parameters, 14B active parameters, and support for a 1M ultra-long context window. The weights are planned for official open-source release **before November 25**; the current version is an intermediate post-training checkpoint. ### 6. Software Engineers' Skill Sets Are Rapidly "De-Technicalizing"

### 7. AI's "Curse of Knowledge" Can Be Circumvented with Multi-Agent Collaboration

**The smarter a single agent gets, the harder it is to simulate an uninformed perspective.** In an article published on Jike, [the author compiles practical thinking on using multi-agent architectures](https://m.okjike.com/originalPosts/6af5589c951be4b7de9d9a8f), noting that the core problem is that once a single agent accumulates a large body of knowledge, it struggles to reason from a beginner's point of view. Multi-agent setups address this through **role specialization**, distributing different cognitive starting points across agents. These insights, drawn from real-world use, are a useful reference for developers designing agent task divisions.
