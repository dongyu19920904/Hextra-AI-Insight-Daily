### 4. [Nvidia Pretrained a 12B Model in 4-bit Precision, Almost Nobody in the Chinese Tech Scene Noticed](https://m.okjike.com/originalPosts/6a0aeb5e657481ea4e529a82)

For years, pretraining has been dominated by 16-bit and 8-bit. You can quantize at inference to save VRAM, but actually pretraining in 4-bit? Gradients blow up, loss crashes—it's basically been the industry's unspoken "impossible." Nvidia broke that assumption with NVFP4, a new format: instead of brutally chopping everything down to 4-bit, they slice numbers into small blocks and give each block its own scale. Result: 2-3x speedup, 50% memory cut, and almost zero intelligence loss. This isn't a minor tweak—it's a paradigm shift in pretraining. Training bigger models might not need as much VRAM anymore.

### 8. [NOVA Framework: Can AI Self-Iteration Actually Discover Real New Knowledge? Is There a Ceiling?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15219)

"Generate, verify, accumulate, retrain"—that's the standard loop for AI self-improvement right now. NOVA models this as an adaptive sampling process, trying to answer a fundamental question: can AI actually discover genuinely new knowledge through iterative self-improvement? What's the cost? The research flags several failure modes, including knowledge contamination and diminishing returns. Not an engineering paper—it's drawing boundaries on AI scaling's ceiling. For people tracking the AGI path, this framework offers a rare theoretical angle.

### 9. [High-Res Image Gen on Mobile? ElasticDiT Squeezed DiT Architecture Into Mobile Devices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15684)

The DiT architecture behind Stable Diffusion 3 and FLUX.1 is the gold standard for high-fidelity image gen, but shipping it to phones was basically impossible—compute and memory overhead too brutal. ElasticDiT uses elastic architecture plus sparse attention to cram it into mobile without obvious quality loss. If this pans out, local high-quality image generation on your phone without cloud API calls becomes real. Privacy, speed, cost—three problems solved at once.

### 10. [Diffusion Model RL Fine-Tuning Doesn't Need Every Step Optimized? Doing Less Actually Works Better](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15855)

Standard approach to preference-aligning diffusion models with RL: hit the entire denoising trajectory with RL—huge compute, results aren't even guaranteed. This paper finds RL impact varies wildly across denoising stages: early stages barely matter, later stages are key. Skip early-stage optimization, save compute, and preference alignment actually gets better. "Less is more" is showing up more and more in AI training—that signal alone is worth remembering.
