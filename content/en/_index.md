### 4. [Nvidia Pretrained a 12B Model in 4-bit Precision, Almost Nobody in China Noticed](https://m.okjike.com/originalPosts/6a0aeb5e657481ea4e529a82)

For years, pretraining was dominated by 16-bit and 8-bit. You could quantize at inference to save VRAM, but actually pretraining in 4-bit? Gradients blow up, loss crashes—basically the industry's unspoken "impossible." Nvidia broke that with NVFP4, a new format: instead of brutally chopping everything to 4-bit, it splits numbers into small blocks, each with its own scale. Result: 2-3x speedup, 50% memory cut, intelligence nearly lossless. This isn't a small optimization—it's a paradigm shift in pretraining. Training bigger models without needing a ton of VRAM might actually be possible now.

### 8. [NOVA Framework: Can AI Self-Iteration Actually Discover Real New Knowledge? Are There Limits?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15219)

"Generate, verify, accumulate, retrain"—standard loop for AI self-improvement. NOVA models this as adaptive sampling, asking a fundamental question: can AI through iterative self-improvement actually discover genuinely new knowledge? What's the cost? Research identifies typical failure modes, including knowledge contamination and diminishing returns. Not an engineering paper—it's drawing boundaries on AI scaling's ceiling. For people tracking the AGI path, this framework offers rare theoretical perspective.

### 9. [High-Res Image Generation on Mobile? ElasticDiT Squeezes DiT Architecture into Mobile Devices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15684)

The DiT architecture behind Stable Diffusion 3 and FLUX.1 is the standard for high-fidelity image generation, but deploying to phones was basically impossible—compute and memory overhead too massive. ElasticDiT uses elastic architecture plus sparse attention, fitting this into mobile without obvious quality loss. If this pans out, local high-quality image generation on phones without cloud API calls becomes real. Privacy, speed, cost—three problems solved at once.

---

### 10. [Diffusion Model RL Fine-Tuning Doesn't Need Every Step Optimized? Doing Less Actually Works Better](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15855)

Standard approach for preference-aligning diffusion models with RL: apply RL across the entire denoising trajectory—heavy compute, results not guaranteed. This paper finds RL impact varies wildly across denoising stages: early stages have minimal impact, later stages are key. Skip early-stage optimization, save compute, preference alignment actually improves. "Less is more" conclusions are popping up more frequently in AI training—that signal itself is worth remembering.
