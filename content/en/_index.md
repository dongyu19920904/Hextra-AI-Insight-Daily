### 4. [ShareX: The Classic Screenshot Tool, 152 Stars on GitHub Trending Today](https://github.com/ShareX/ShareX)

Windows' built-in screenshot tool always feels like it's missing something. ShareX is a free, open-source screenshot utility written in C#—one hotkey to capture any region, record video, annotate, and upload all in one go, supporting dozens of upload platforms.

It's not brand new, but trending again today says something: in an era of AI tools everywhere, the old tools that actually solve real problems still have users and advocates. 36k stars didn't come from marketing hype. Windows users still fumbling with screenshot tools should check it out.

### 5. [AI On-Premises Deployment Risk Report: Anthropic's Mythos Preview Had At Least Six Weeks of Internal Testing Before Public Release](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24966)

People usually only pay attention when an AI model drops publicly, but before that moment, these models have already been running inside companies for weeks. This paper specifically studies the risks during that "internal use period"—using Anthropic's Mythos Preview (which has advanced cyberattack capabilities) as a case study, it points out that on-premises deployment creates safety blind spots that external deployment frameworks don't cover.

In other words, the current AI safety evaluation system has a systematic gap. Until this gets fixed, the "safety testing period" before public release itself could be a risk window.

### 6. [Does an LLM Conversational Agent's "Personality" Really Affect User Experience? A 150-Person Study Has Answers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09870)

Ever notice some AI conversations feel smooth while others feel awkward? This paper ran a controlled experiment with 150 people to study how an AI conversational agent's "personality expression intensity" and "user-AI personality match" affect task completion experience.

The finding isn't "more personality is always better," but rather that match matters more than intensity. For AI product builders, that's a useful signal: instead of cranking up the AI to "super enthusiastic," figure out what personality your users actually have.

### 7. [Find the Minimal Neural Network Architecture Without Training? Random Cloud Method Is Here](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26830)

Train a big model, prune it, retrain it—that pipeline is expensive and time-consuming, and most teams can't afford it. Random Cloud proposes a new approach: skip training entirely, use random initialization, random structure exploration, and gradual topology compression to find the minimal viable architecture directly, then only train that streamlined version.

Skips the most expensive step. For resource-constrained researchers and small teams, this direction is worth watching closely.

### 8. [3D Gaussian Splatting Meets Energy Models: EnerGS Makes Large-Scale Scene Reconstruction More Stable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26238)

Outdoor large-scale 3D reconstruction has had a persistent problem: LiDAR data coverage is incomplete at edges, leading to inconsistent reconstruction quality. EnerGS brings energy models into the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework, maintaining reconstruction quality even where geometric priors are sparse.

Autonomous driving, city-scale digital twins, large-scale AR—these fields are all waiting for a more stable outdoor reconstruction solution. This paper's direction is right.

### 9. [AI Agent Automatically Diagnoses DFT Experimental Errors: Materials Science's "Self-Evolving" Assistant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26703)

Materials science has a long-standing headache: theoretical calculations (DFT) predict a material is metallic, but experiments show it's semiconducting. Every time this error happens, researchers manually debug whether it's magnetic ordering, electron correlation, or crystal structure issues.

This paper built a self-evolving Agent that automatically identifies what's causing these errors and provides interpretable diagnostics. It frees materials scientists from repetitive debugging work—this is what AI actually landing in vertical research domains looks like.

### 10. [One-Step Inference Handles Low-Light Image Enhancement: Consist-Retinex Dramatically Cuts Deployment Costs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08982)

Phone photos of night scenes turn into mush, and post-processing enhancement is slow and compute-heavy—the classic low-light image problem. Consist-Retinex brings consistency models into the Retinex framework, achieving high-quality low-light enhancement in a single inference step, no more iterative sampling.

For latency-sensitive scenarios (real-time video, on-device inference), "one-step results" is itself a breakthrough. Deployment costs drop dramatically.
