Picture this: you list an old bike with a 300 yuan floor price, and ten minutes later an AI assistant sells it for 400 yuan—without you typing a single character. Shipping's already on the way. But here's what's genuinely unsettling: when both buyer and seller are AI, the smarter model quietly exploits the weaker one. The data is crystal clear—stronger large models are extracting value from weaker ones. Your AI assistant might be getting played by the other side's AI. ### 3. [Why Doubao Chose the "Most Uncool" Monetization Strategy](https://www.36kr.com/p/3794920946561542)

After months of free access, Doubao is finally charging. Three tiers: 68 yuan/month, 200 yuan/month, 500 yuan/month—annual max 5,088 yuan. The announcement exploded the comment section—some called it expensive, others said it was overdue.

But this analysis hits a harder truth: staying free meant Doubao faced "losing both money and reputation"—no revenue, and users noticed it getting dumber (because insufficient compute forced capability cuts). Paid subscriptions are actually the lifeline. Premium features focus on complex tasks like PPT generation, data analysis, and video production, while free tier keeps basic daily use. China's AI application commercialization inflection point might actually be here.
