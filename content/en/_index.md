## **🔥 Top 10 Must-Read**

### 1. [VS Code Quietly Added Copilot as "Co-Author" to Commits—Developers Are Furious](https://the-decoder.com/microsoft-adds-hidden-copilot-co-author-to-vs-code-commits-sparking-backlash/)

Microsoft silently added a hidden `Co-Authored-By` tag for Copilot-generated commits in VS Code. Looks like a minor tweak, but it hit developers where it hurts: who actually wrote the code, who's responsible, and whether tools should pollute the historical record.

As AI coding gets more mainstream, version control stops being just a technical log and becomes a liability log. Today's backlash sends a clear message: developers are fine with AI helping, but not with tools rewriting their commit history.

### 2. [Xiaomi Open-Sources MiMo-V2.5-Pro, Targeting Long-Duration Autonomous Coding](https://the-decoder.com/xiaomis-open-weight-mimo-v2-5-pro-takes-aim-at-claude-opus-with-hours-long-autonomous-coding/)

MiMo-V2.5-Pro isn't just another chat model—Xiaomi dropped it with open weights, built for extended autonomous coding, and directly benchmarked against high-end closed-source models.

What matters here is the shift: domestic models aren't just chasing general Q&A anymore—they're pushing hard on "can actually keep working" engineering capabilities. For developers, the future means more choices for local and private coding assistants.

### 3. [OpenAI Quietly Enabled Marketing Cookies for Free ChatGPT Users by Default](https://the-decoder.com/openai-defaults-to-marketing-cookies-for-free-chatgpt-users/)

ChatGPT stopped being just a chat tool—it's now an entry point, account system, ad opportunity, and user behavior goldmine. OpenAI defaulting free users into marketing cookies puts "how does free AI actually make money" right on the table.

For regular users, the real takeaway isn't the cookies themselves—it's the reminder that free AI services have business models too. Going forward, privacy settings on AI tools will look more like browsers and social platforms: you'll need to check them yourself.

### 4. [xAI Lets Grok Users Clone Their Voice—But Added Verification First](https://the-decoder.com/xai-now-lets-you-clone-your-voice-for-grok-with-verification/)

Voice cloning is a killer feature, but it's also a security nightmare. xAI now lets users clone their voice for Grok, but requires verification—a sign that big tech already knows this line can't be crossed without guardrails.

Once this scales, AI voices become identity assets. Being able to prove "this voice clone was authorized by me" might matter more than sounding identical. That's the real shift.

### 5. [Anthropic Brings Claude for Enterprise to the U.S. Congress](https://llm-stats.com/ai-news)

Anthropic just put Claude for Enterprise in the hands of U.S. congressional staff and lawmakers. This isn't just selling software—it's a signal that AI companies are entering public-sector workflows.

Government use cases demand compliance, audit trails, and strict permission boundaries. If Claude can run in Congress, enterprises will feel more confident that "AI assistants can handle serious work."

### 6. [Meta and IBM Built an AI That Can Rewrite Its Own Code](https://llm-stats.com/ai-news)

Meta and IBM's prototype pulls "self-improvement" from sci-fi into engineering: let AI watch its own performance, then try tweaking the code. Sounds radical, but the real challenge is verifying each change doesn't drift the system off course.

This made Top 10 because it points to the next phase of Agents: not just executing tasks, but maintaining and optimizing their own toolchains. Once automation gets here, testing, sandboxing, and rollback become core infrastructure.

### 9. [New Benchmark: Large Models Flunk Simple Ethical Dilemmas](https://the-decoder.com/new-benchmark-reveals-llms-often-fail-simple-ethical-dilemmas/)

This matters for enterprise rollouts. Customer service, healthcare, education, hiring—you can't just test accuracy. You need to test boundaries, refusals, and consistency. The more human-like AI gets, the more you need to validate it like a person, not a tool.
