## **🔥 Top 10 Today**

### 1. GPT-6 Astra Cracks Napoleon's Cipher in Six Hours

**A 217-year cold case, solved.** An AI engineer fed GPT-6 Astra a photo of Napoleon's 1809 encrypted letter, and [GPT-6 Astra cracked Napoleon's cipher in six hours](https://www.36kr.com/p/4007160336027525). This letter had topped Cryptiana's unsolved list for years—only **1202×1836 pixels**, each symbol averaging just **19 pixels**. The AI decoded troop deployment maps and even recovered half a sentence redacted from Napoleon's memoirs for 160 years.

### 7. Micron CEO Forecasts Tighter Memory Supply in 2027-2028

**AI training is cranking up storage demand.** Micron's CEO told investors on the Q4 earnings call that [memory supply will be much tighter in 2027 and 2028 than in 2026](https://www.techpowerup.com/353296/micron-ceo-says-memory-supply-will-be-much-tighter-in-2027-and-2028-than-in-2026). Over **75%** of Micron's 2027 output is already committed to customers, and the Idaho ID2 fab won't start wafer production until **late 2028**💾. DRAM prices rose high double-digits this quarter. Companies planning AI clusters or bulk buys need to lock in supply early.

### 8. Brain Traveling Wave Research Reveals Complex Spatiotemporal Coordination

**Waves aren't just background noise.** A **Nature Communications** study from **April 2026** used intracranial electrodes in epilepsy patients to map [brain traveling waves revealing complex spatiotemporal coordination patterns](https://www.quantamagazine.org/surprisingly-complex-waves-reveal-the-brains-inner-workings-20260930/)🌊. Virtual navigation tasks triggered more **rotating waves** than word recall; forward-backward propagation direction correlated with memory encoding and retrieval switching. The findings don't settle causality yet, but offer fresh angles on short-term brain coordination.
