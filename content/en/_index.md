## **🔥 Today's Top 10 Focus**

### 1. GPT-6 Astra Cracks Napoleon's Cipher in Six Hours

**A 217-year cold case solved.** An AI engineer fed GPT-6 Astra a photo of Napoleon's 1809 encrypted letter, [and GPT-6 Astra cracked Napoleon's cipher in six hours](https://www.36kr.com/p/4007160336027525). This letter had dominated Cryptiana's unsolved list; the image resolution was only **1202×1836 pixels**, with each symbol averaging just **19 pixels**. The AI decoded troop deployment diagrams and restored half a decree that had been redacted from Napoleon's memoirs for 160 years.

### 7. Micron CEO Predicts Tighter Memory Supply in 2027-2028

**AI training drives storage demand higher.** Micron's CEO stated on the Q4 fiscal year call that [memory supply will be much tighter in 2027 and 2028 than in 2026](https://www.techpowerup.com/353296/micron-ceo-says-memory-supply-will-be-much-tighter-in-2027-and-2028-than-in-2026). Micron has already committed **over 75%** of its 2027 output to customers, and the Idaho ID2 fab won't start wafer production until **late 2028** 💾. DRAM prices rose by high-teen percentages this quarter. Companies planning AI clusters or large-scale procurement need to lock in supply early.

### 8. Brain Traveling Wave Research Reveals Complex Spatiotemporal Coordination Patterns

**Oscillations aren't just background noise.** Research published in Nature Communications in **April 2026** used intracranial electrodes in epilepsy patients to [reveal surprisingly complex spatiotemporal coordination patterns in brain traveling waves](https://www.quantamagazine.org/surprisingly-complex-waves-reveal-the-brains-inner-workings-20260930/) 🌊. Virtual navigation tasks produced **rotating waves** more often than word recall, and forward-backward propagation direction correlated with memory encoding and retrieval switching. The findings don't yet establish causality but offer fresh perspectives on short-term brain coordination.
